## 1. What the board is being asked

**The question:** Is the Readiness Ladder (defined in section 3) sound as the acquisition and revenue spine of vooee? Stress-test the conversion assumptions, the pricing, the sequencing against the AIPT deal, solo capacity, and brand readiness.

**What the board is NOT being asked:** whether this is "one more product." That objection was litigated in the 22 July session and it does not survive contact with the structure. This is not an addition to the product list; it is a consolidation of it:

- The free Readiness Check absorbs and replaces the "AI Ground Rules Check" (doc 07, rung 0).
- The $129 AI Policy Pack stops being a competing SKU and becomes a bonus inside the paid Snapshot.
- The Alignment Room is not new; it is the existing $1,900 Working Session, renamed and given a repeatable mechanic.
- Everything above that ($18k Sprint, $15k 90-Day Engagement, $8,500/month Fractional) already exists on the live site.

One rung is genuinely new: the Leadership Alignment Snapshot ($99-149 + GST). Everything else is the existing ladder with a front door attached.

**Jeanna's strategic claim, which the board should test rather than dismiss:** this ladder moves vooee out of the general AI-influencer content lane (tips, prompts, tool round-ups, a crowded and depreciating space) and into a business-strategist position. The product is leadership perception-gap data and governance facilitation. Nobody else in the AU SMB market holds that dataset or that mechanic. Challenge whether the claim holds, not whether the ladder should exist.

## 5. Where the founder wants to be challenged

These are the honest weak points. Push here, not on product-count theology:

1. **B2B sharing physics.** Executives do not share quizzes like consumers share star signs. The invite loop is intentional sharing, better suited to this buyer, but unproven. What is the minimum viable test before any spend?
2. **Conversion assumptions.** Free check → Snapshot → Room has three conversion steps, each unmeasured. What numbers would make the ladder self-funding, and by when should they exist?
3. **Solo capacity.** Facilitation days are the scarce resource. If the ladder works, rungs 2-4 compete for the same calendar. Where does it break, and what is the pressure valve (pricing up, waitlist, partner delivery)?
4. **Sequencing risk (the Kohler echo).** Building the quiz before AIPT has signed is another packaging move with zero engine sales. Standing decision: proposal ships first; quiz build decisions land the week AIPT signs. Test whether that discipline is holding or slipping.
5. **Brand dependency.** The front door is consumer-grade and the visual identity is unresolved. Does the quiz launch wait for the brand, or does a good-enough interim ship?
6. **Open compliance items.** Both IP checks, archetype name collision check, not-legal-advice line on the quiz.
