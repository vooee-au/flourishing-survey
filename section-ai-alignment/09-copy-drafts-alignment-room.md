**Start here. The Alignment Room. $1,900 AUD.**
Half a day with your leadership team, run as a live session on your own devices. Everyone answers the same questions in silence first: what you actually want AI for, what worries you, which decisions should always stay with a person. Then the answers go up on the screen, side by side.

Most leadership teams discover they have five different answers to "why do we want AI?". That is normal, and it is the reason AI projects stall. By the end of the half day you have one answer, written in your own words, along with a 90-day roadmap and the first draft of your AI ground rules, built live in the room from what your team said.

For businesses that want AI but can't yet say why, this is the half day that answers it. And if the honest answer in parts of your business is "we shouldn't", the roadmap says that too.

## Piece 3: AIPT / Foundation Education proposal skeleton

*(Word document, Foundation Education branding courtesy, sent to Jordan. Prices and options marked [J] are Jeanna's decisions before sending.)*

**1. What we heard on Tuesday.**
Three things from the demo session, played back plainly. Your team wants Claude working in sales and marketing now, without waiting for the HubSpot migration. You want the team able to build and run this themselves, without depending on me. And the room surfaced a genuinely useful question about scope: sales and marketing first, but where do the student journey and operations fit, and when? That question deserves a proper answer from your leadership team, and there's a good way to get it.

**2. The shape I recommend.**
Two parts, in order.

*Part one: the Alignment Room (leadership, half a day).* Your leaders each answer the same questions independently in the room, then we put the answers side by side and settle the scope question properly: what AI is for at FEG and AIPT, what order to do things in, which decisions stay with people, and the ground rules for the team. You leave with a written 90-day roadmap and a drafted AI policy. This is also where "sales and marketing first" gets pressure-tested against everything else on the table, so the whole leadership team owns the answer.

*Part two: the AI Enablement Sprint (the sales and marketing team, six weeks).* A cohort of five. Week one covers foundations and safe use, week two locks each person's project (drawn from the real sales and marketing work: the dashboards, the content engine, the nurture emails we looked at), weeks three to five are individual coaching, week six the cohort shows what they built. Leadership gets a report on capability before and after, what the team built, and what to focus on next. The sprint is how "the team runs it without Jeanna" actually happens.
