# Could you launch Section in Australia? An honest assessment
Compiled 22 July 2026.

## Who they are
Section (sectionai.com, formerly Section4/Section School), New York based, founded around Scott Galloway, led by CEO Greg Shove. They sell enterprise AI upskilling: cohort courses, a proficiency benchmark, an AI adoption platform (ProfAI), and bootcamps like the Head of AI program. Their engine is free star-studded events feeding a B2B sales motion. Their stated ambition has been explicitly about scale ("100,000 students into the AI class").

## The case for
- They are US/EU-centric with global demand; APAC time zones genuinely hurt live-cohort businesses. A local delivery partner solves a real operational problem.
- Australian enterprise demand is documented (the same EY data you already cite). No dominant local AI-upskilling brand has emerged yet.
- Your profile fits the brief unusually well: led AI partnerships at Amazon Alexa AU/NZ, MIT agentic AI program, AWS accreditation, a working AI practice, and you can actually teach. That is a rare combination for a country hire.

## The case against, honestly
- Section's model is software plus content at scale. Companies like that open sales offices, and mostly they just sell remotely into new markets. A "country launcher" role may simply never exist; if it does, it is a sales leadership job with a quota, which is a different life from the one vooee is building.
- Taking their flag makes you the local face of an enterprise ChatGPT/Copilot brand while vooee's differentiation is SMB, hands-on, Claude-native. It could eat your calendar, your positioning, and your two fractional seats.
- You would be building their brand equity in AU, not yours, at exactly the moment vooee's ladder has started working.

## The middle path (recommended)
Aim for partnership, not employment. Three rungs, lowest risk first:
1. **Become a visible, useful peer.** Download and genuinely engage with their research; publish the AU SMB commentary they cannot write (pillar 3 of the social strategy). Two or three sharp, cited posts that tag their research will get you on their radar the honest way.
2. **Bring them AU data.** Once the Sprint proficiency pulse (doc 02) has a few cohorts of Australian numbers, you hold something their research team wants. That is a warm door-opener to a genuine conversation.
3. **Propose a scoped pilot.** An "APAC delivery partner" or licensed-facilitator arrangement for one cohort: they keep the brand and curriculum, you deliver in AU hours and keep vooee. If that works, a bigger conversation about the market happens with you already inside it.

Shape: one line on who you are (Alexa AU/NZ partnerships, MIT agentic AI, running an AI advisory in Sydney), one line of evidence you know their model (reference the Proficiency Report finding, with a view on how AU differs), one ask ("if APAC is on the roadmap in the next year, I'd like to be in that conversation"). ## Bottom line
Plausible, but not as a cold "let me open your office" pitch, and not worth trading vooee's momentum for a quota. Work the peer-then-partner path; it costs you three LinkedIn posts and some Sprint data you should be collecting anyway, and it leaves every door open.
