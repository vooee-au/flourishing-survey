# Part A · The workbook

## How to use this workbook
One copy, owned by one person, written in by the whole leadership team. Sections 1 and 2 are filled in during the Working Session. Sections 3 to 5 are worked through across the ninety days. Section 6 is yours to keep running after that. If a page stays blank, that is information too; bring it to the next working session.

## Section 3 · Build and prove (days 31 to 60)

### Choosing what to build
For each candidate project, answer five questions before anyone opens a tool. The fifth is the one that saves you money.
1. What problem are we solving?
2. What value does it create, and how would we measure it?
3. What capability does it build in our people?
4. What risk does it carry, and can we govern it with the grid above?
5. What are we deliberately choosing *not* to pursue this quarter?

### Where the effort goes
AI effort tends to pool at individual productivity and stall there. Note where each project sits; make sure at least one lives above the first row.

| Level | Project | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| One person's work | | |
| A team's work | | |
| A whole function | | |
| The business itself | | |

### The four tests
Before trusting any tool with something that matters. Skip for bottom-left-grid uses.

| Test | The question | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | Does it do the job? | Ten real examples from our business, done correctly |
| Compliance | Does it follow our rules? | Data handled per our policy; outputs meet our sector's requirements |
| Adversarial | What happens at the edges? | Fed the weird cases, it fails safely, not confidently |
| Scenario | Does it survive a real week? | Run in parallel with the normal process before switching |

Keep a one-line record of what was tested. That record is what makes diligence visible to a client, an insurer, or an adviser.

### The security check
Ten questions, yes / no / not sure. Every no and not sure goes on the action list. (Identify: do we know every tool in use, and what data each can see? Protect: strong passwords and MFA on work AI accounts; sensitive data kept out of unapproved tools. Detect: would we notice a tool doing something wrong; does someone check automations on a schedule? Respond: does everyone know who to tell, same day; do we know when a slip becomes a notifiable breach? Recover: could we operate with the tool switched off; after an incident, do we change something?)

## Section 4 · Make it stick (days 61 to 90)

### The routine
- Every client-facing output read by a person who owns it. No exceptions; this is the one rule that never bends.
- Spot-check frequencies from the grid (weekly for drafts, monthly for automations).
- A quarterly look at what tools have access to; a policy review every six months.

### The bad day
Four moves, on the wall: **stop → tell → assess → fix and record.** Pre-brief the adviser on when a data slip becomes a notifiable breach under the Privacy Act; deadlines apply, and mid-incident is the wrong time to learn them.

### Champions and the library
- Who are our two or three AI champions (the people others actually ask)? ______________
- Where do working prompts, projects and workflows live so they outlast the person who built them? ______________
- What is our version of showing the work: a monthly show-and-tell, a shared channel, five minutes in the team meeting? ______________

### The numbers we will watch
Enterprise benchmarks, translated to our size (Section, 2026, cited for context, not worship):
- **Using it weekly:** how many of us touch AI in real work every week? (Their bar: 80% of the workforce.)
- **Using it well:** how many could show someone else a working workflow, not just a chat window? (Their bar: 80% proficient, 20% expert.)
- **Something always running:** does each person have at least one task quietly handled in the background?
