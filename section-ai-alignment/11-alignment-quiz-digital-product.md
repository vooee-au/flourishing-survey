### What the numbers say
1. **No Lauren sweet spot exists** (>10,000 volume, <50 KD): AU tops out at 480/mo; the one big US term (ai ethics and governance, 22k) is KD74 and owned by certification bodies. Same verdict as the Policy Pack in July: search will not carry this product.
2. **But the astrology blueprint doesn't rank for anything either.** That funnel runs on social audience and quiz mechanics, not Google. Jeanna's instinct ("this is something I can build an audience on") is the correct reading of the data: audience-first, search-agnostic.
3. **The clicks competitors buy are expensive** ($8-27 CPC; "ai policy template" at $146.87 in AU is extraordinary). Every email the quiz captures has real commercial value; agencies and platforms pay dearly for this exact intent.
4. **The best ratio terms are the assessment ones** (ai readiness assessment US 880/KD33; ai governance assessment US 1,000/KD20): small but cheap to rank for, and exactly what the product is. Worth owning as the landing page's SEO layer, treated as a bonus channel, not the plan.

## What to steal from the astrology funnel (mechanics, not content)
- **Identity first.** The product is about YOU, not about AI. The astrology version personalises via natal chart; ours personalises via your answers and, crucially, your guesses about your co-leaders.
- **Instant, tangible artefact.** A designed personal report the moment you finish, not "check your email in 3 days".
- **The free-ChatGPT objection, pre-answered.** Their page argues why the paid version beats asking ChatGPT; ours has a better answer: ChatGPT cannot tell you what your co-leaders actually think. The data is the moat, not the prompts.
- **Low-ticket ascension.** Free thing, cheap thing, expensive thing, each obviously feeding the next.

## The product: the perception-gap quiz
The mechanic nobody else has, lifted straight from the Alignment Room: each leader answers twice — how AI sits with **you**, and how you believe your **fellow leaders** would answer. The gap between those two is the product.

**Tier 0, free: the individual quiz (lead magnet).** ~10 questions, 5 minutes. Instant personal report: your AI leadership archetype (identity, shareable), your confidence and worry profile, your predicted team gap, one practical next step. Email-gated report, joins the Brief list. The archetype layer is the astrology ingredient: The Accelerator, The Guardian, The Translator, The Measurer — names for how you lead on AI (final set to be written properly; Ikigai already proved the archetype pattern works for vooee).
**Built-in referral loop:** the report's natural CTA is "You think your leadership team disagrees on X. Invite them and find out." Each completion recruits the rest of a leadership team. The quiz spreads sideways through organisations, which is audience-building with intent, not reach.

**Tier 1, paid: the Leadership Alignment Snapshot ($99-$149 + GST).** Unlocked when 3+ leaders from one business complete it: the aggregated report — real gaps vs perceived gaps, the confidence spread, the five-answers-to-why moment, a draft governance statement template, and the line that sells the room: "You now know where you disagree. Resolving it is a half day." Credited against the $1,900 Alignment Room within 60 days (same bridge as doc 07).

**Tier 2: the facilitated Alignment Room ($1,900), then the ladder as built.**

The self-serve report deliberately ends at *diagnosis*. It names the tensions; it does not resolve them. Resolution is the facilitated product. That line protects the $1,900 exactly the way doc 07's "documents vs transformation" line protects the engagement.

## Pricing logic
- Don't charge for the individual quiz: charging kills the referral loop, and the loop is the growth engine.
- Charge at the team layer, where value is undeniable and the buyer is a business, not a person. $99-$149 is impulse-approvable on a company card, materially above the $129 pack (which can become a bonus inside the Snapshot rather than a competing SKU).
- The astrology blueprint sells at consumer low-ticket; B2B equivalents price the aggregation, not the entertainment. The Snapshot is a diagnostic a CEO forwards to their board, which is worth more than $27.

## Why this is the audience play (Jeanna's thesis, endorsed)
- The Scorecard proves she can run check-to-report funnels; Ikigai proves the archetype quiz pattern; the Alignment Room provides the questions and the brand story. This is assembly, not invention.
- Every governance-flavoured social post (pillars 3 and 4, doc 03) gets a conversion object that isn't a checkout: "take the quiz, see your gap."
- The aggregated, anonymised data becomes proprietary content within months: "across N Australian leadership teams, the average confidence gap is X; 7 in 10 leaders think their peers are more optimistic than they are." Nobody in AU has that dataset. It feeds LinkedIn, the Brief, PR, and eventually the Section conversation (doc 04's "bring them AU data" play).

## Honest cautions
1. **The Kohler echo.** This is another packaging move with zero engine sales. Sequence: AIPT proposal ships this week regardless; quiz build decisions land "the week AIPT signs" alongside the pack decision the board already scheduled. The quiz is the doc 07 rung 0, upgraded, not a new commitment queue-jumping the invoice.
2. **B2B sharing physics.** Executives don't share quizzes like consumers share star signs. The invite-your-team loop replaces public sharing with intentional sharing, which is better suited to this buyer, but it needs testing before scaling spend.
3. **One quiz brand, not three.** Ikigai (individual work), Scorecard (website), Alignment Quiz (leadership). Distinct jobs, but the site's Resources shelf must present them as one family or the brand reads scattered.
4. **Names and claims.** No "constitution" language client-facing (Allie Miller check still open); archetype names need a collision check; and the quiz must carry the same not-legal-advice line as the pack.
